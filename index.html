Roofing Job Checker

Before you pay, sign, or feel pressured to decide: make sure you know exactly what roofing work is included, what it costs, and what happens next.

The key lesson

An invoice is not always a proper breakdown of the job.

A single total can hide several different tasks. Ask for each part of the work to be described clearly, with its own price where practical, before you agree. Keep the quote, scope of work, invoice and any changes together.

What will they do?

List each repair or replacement, its location and the materials or finish expected.

What will it cost?

Show labour, materials, scaffolding, waste removal, VAT if applicable, and the total.

What is not included?

Identify exclusions, provisional sums, possible extras and what needs approval first.

1Before you agree or pay

2Don't let urgency replace clear details

A genuine roof leak can need prompt action. Even so, urgency should not mean you have to guess what you're buying.

If someone says “we need to take the scaffolding away” or pushes you to pay immediately: pause and ask for the exact work, full price, payment terms and any scaffolding costs in writing. Ask what genuinely needs doing now and what can wait. Don't sign or pay for work you don't understand simply because you feel under pressure.

If immediate temporary work is needed to make the property safe, ask for a written description and price for that emergency work separately from any later, larger repair. If you cannot get clear answers, consider getting another written quote or independent advice before committing, where the situation allows.

3Warning signs to slow down and check

  • The quote only says “roof repair” or gives a single figure without describing the tasks.
  • Several different jobs are bundled together, but no one explains what each includes.
  • The price or work changes verbally and you are not given an updated written breakdown.
  • You are told to pay immediately or risk losing scaffolding, without a clear explanation of the options and costs.
  • You are asked for a large upfront payment and the reason and payment stages are unclear.
  • You are promised a guarantee, but the terms, duration or who provides it are not documented.

One warning sign alone doesn't prove a company is dishonest. Use these as prompts to ask questions, compare quotes and keep a clear record.

Already paid or signed?

Keep all paperwork and messages, take dated photos if safe, and write down what you believe was agreed versus what was done. Ask the company for a written itemisation and explanation of any difference. Avoid making accusations before you have the facts.

For UK consumer guidance, see Citizens Advice consumer advice. The right options can depend on how the agreement was made, when it was made, the urgency and the facts, so seek tailored advice if there is a dispute.

Roofing Job Checker is general information, not legal advice or an assessment of any specific contractor. It cannot verify a roofer's competence or guarantee an outcome. If there is an active leak or immediate safety risk, take reasonable steps to protect people and property while seeking qualified help.
